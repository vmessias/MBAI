Faces of the Gap: Inequality in Brazil’s Public Schools

A storyboard for a scrollytelling piece that follows students, families, and teachers through one school week to reveal how public–private and regional gaps shape learning conditions and outcomes.

RoleNarrative, research, storyboard design

AudienceFamilies, teachers, local administrators

FormatScroll-based visual essay

Concept & narrative spine

Instead of testing a formal hypothesis, this project uses narrative beats and visual prototypes to surface where inequality shows up in K–12: commute, classroom rhythm, community support, and final results.

Each scene combines a short title, a neutral annotation, and one primary visual anchored in three consistent school types: municipal public, state public, and private.

Scenes · One week in school

Storyboard
  1. 01

    Before the first bell

    Commute and readiness

    The week begins on the road: some students cross long distances to reach urban schools, arriving drained before class even starts.

    Student voice
    “If the bus is late, I miss the first activity and spend the day trying to catch up.”

    Takeaway
    Time and energy lost in transit quietly shape who arrives ready to learn and who starts the week behind.

    Visualization idea
    Narrative illustration of the path home → bus stop → bus → school; no numbers, just composition and light to emphasize distance and fatigue.

    Illustration · Commute journey
  2. 02

    When class finally starts

    Late arrivals and fragmented focus

    At the first bell, some notebooks are open while others are still settling. Attention is out of sync across the room.

    Teacher voice
    “Half the class is ready; the rest needs the first minutes just to understand what is going on.”

    Takeaway
    Longer-than-recommended commutes correlate with lower concentration and slower warm‑up at the start of lessons.

    Visualization idea
    Two small-multiple bar charts: share of on‑time vs. late arrivals, and focused vs. catching‑up students, by school type.

    Small multiples · Arrival & focus
  3. 03

    Two classrooms, two starting lines

    Public vs private momentum

    In one room, new content begins with everyone present; in another, time is consumed revisiting material and equalizing gaps.

    Teacher voice
    “Private schools have more tools, and students arrive less exhausted by basic constraints.”

    Takeaway
    Structural conditions make it easier for private-school classes to advance the syllabus while public schools often work reactively.

    Visualization idea
    Line chart of learning scores over years by school type, with a single callout at the widest gap and a neutral annotation.

    Line chart · Scores over time
  4. 04

    When thirty feels like forty

    Class size and rhythm

    The clock moves at the same speed, but comprehension checks take longer in crowded rooms.

    Teacher voice
    “With large classes, every interruption expands; catching everyone up changes the whole tempo of the lesson.”

    Takeaway
    Bigger classes raise the cost of responding to divergence, making proactive teaching harder.

    Visualization idea
    Two side-by-side vignettes: a small class and a large class, same teacher, same time, different density of students; no numbers, just scale.

    Illustration · Small vs large class
  5. 05

    After school, the learning doesn’t stop

    Community support vs. absence

    Outside the classroom, some students find quiet study spaces and support; others juggle chores, noise, and adult worries.

    Parents & guardians
    “When there is full‑day school or a safe study room, children are shielded from part of what we face as adults.”

    Takeaway
    Community infrastructure—libraries, tutoring, safe centers—translates directly into continuity and belonging.

    Visualization idea
    Split scene: on the left, improvised study in a noisy home; on the right, a welcoming community study room with warm light and supervision.

    Diptych · Home vs study center
  6. 06

    By Friday, the gap is visible

    Results distribution

    Where readiness and continuity are higher, proficiency bands cluster differently across school types.

    School coordinator
    “Consistency in time-on-task echoes through a student’s week, year, and life.”

    Takeaway
    Public and private schools end the week with distinct performance profiles that mirror earlier conditions.

    Visualization idea
    Simple band-score bars for public vs private with a subtle “2×” callout at the largest separation, no dense labels.

    Bar chart · Band scores
  7. 07

    One week, three perspectives

    Conditions × outcomes

    The storyboard closes with a synthesis: student, family, and teacher personas plus a matrix connecting conditions and results.

    Takeaway
    Where commute, class size, and community support differ, patterns in readiness and proficiency tend to diverge too—an invitation to redesign, not to end the debate.

    Visualization idea
    Three compact persona cards above an icon/gradient matrix (rows = conditions, columns = outcomes × school type), no numbers, just pattern.

    Personas + Conditions × Outcomes matrix
