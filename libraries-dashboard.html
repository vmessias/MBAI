Rethinking Library Metrics in Ontario

A dashboard concept built on 25 years of public library statistics — more than 9,800 records across 200+ municipalities — to compare funding and use with normalized KPIs instead of misleading raw totals.

Dataset1999–2024 Ontario public library statistics, 142–336 variables per year.

FocusEquity, funding intensity, and usage patterns across regions and time.

RoleData cleaning, EDA, dashboard layout and interaction design.

The data challenge

Why a dashboard?

The raw dataset is almost complete in terms of missing values, but extremely skewed: in some metrics the mean is tens of times larger than the median, driven by outliers like Toronto, which makes traditional averages hide most libraries.

Column names and definitions also change over time — there is effectively 0% column overlap between 2006 and 2024 — so any 25‑year view first requires reconciling schemas and re‑defining stable KPIs before visualization.

Dashboard concept

One view, four questions

Fiscal Gap $21 Real per capita funding loss since 2002.
Efficiency Shock 3.7x Spike in cost-per-visit during pandemic closures.
Wealthiest Segment Outliers Small, wealthy towns spend 2x more per capita.

The Four Realities: Segmentation Analysis

Clustering libraries by population and expenditure reveals four distinct operating realities: Urban Systems, Vulnerable centers, Insolvent rural libraries, and Wealthy Outliers.

The Funding Illusion

Nominal municipal support has risen steadily (dotted line), but after adjusting for inflation (red line), real purchasing power per resident has stagnated or fallen since 2002.

The Efficiency Shock

Cost per visit remained stable for decades until the pandemic shock. While recovering, the cost structure has not fully returned to the pre-2019 baseline.

Diverging Fortunes

Real expenditure trends show a widening gap: Wealthy Outliers (green) maintain high spending, while Insolvent (red) and Vulnerable (yellow) systems face slow erosion.

Design rationale

The layout separates controls and normalized KPIs on the left from visual views on the right, encouraging readers to think in terms of per‑resident investment rather than raw totals. A single conceptual filter state controls all views, so funding, equity, and long‑term trends stay in sync.

Each visualization encodes one of the main EDA findings: skewness and outliers (scatter), hidden leaders when we normalize by population (ranking), the divergence between rising funding and shrinking physical circulation (index chart), and the extent to which the mean can misrepresent typical libraries (gap plot).
